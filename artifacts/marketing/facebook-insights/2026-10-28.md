# Facebook host insights — 2026-10-28

First run. Sources: three public host groups read without joining (Airbnb Host Australia; Hosts Helping Hosts; AirBnB Hosts - Tips & Tricks). About 82 feed posts collected, roughly 50 of them real host discussion (the rest vendor spam, overseas listing ads or guests). Mostly posts from the last 1-3 days, so this covers less than the full 7 days.

## Summary

- **One topic dominated every group: Airbnb's upcoming house-rule / checkout policy (from November).** Hosts say they will no longer be able to *require* checkout chores (dishes, taking out bins, stripping beds), charge late-checkout or party fines in house rules, or collect guest IDs / signed contracts. Listing wording is being flagged automatically. Roughly 29 of ~50 real posts were about this, with anger, confusion over wording, and "what do I write now?" questions.
- **That opens a real but delicate angle for DigiStayBook:** hosts still need to explain *how the house works* (where the bins go, how the dishwasher runs, what to do with keys) without it reading as a list of mandatory chores. A clear, friendly, optional "leaving the house" guide on the Guest Wall fits; pitching it as a way around Airbnb's rules does not.
- **Strong-fit "make the stay feel personal" talk is present but small** (1-2 posts, e.g. a host asking what others do to make stays special: flowers, homemade treats). Most other volume is pricing, cleaners, guest behaviour and platform support, which DigiStayBook doesn't address.

## Marketing suggestions

1. **Landing-page angle: "Explain your house, not your chores."** With hosts suddenly unsure what they may write in listings, frame the Guest Wall as where you explain *how the place works*: bin night, the dishwasher's quirk, where keys go, wifi. Written as friendly help, not obligations. Possible headline: *"Everything your guests need to know, in one scan. Written the way you'd say it."* Keep copy clear that DigiStayBook doesn't enforce house rules or replace the platform's terms.
2. **Free lead magnet: "Friendly checkout guide: wording that helps without demanding."** A one-page PDF / blog post of polite, optional phrasing ("If you have a moment, the dishwasher tablets are under the sink. Our cleaner takes care of the rest"). Also how to split *house information* (always useful) from *rules* (belong in the listing). Ask the operator to check the final wording against Airbnb's published policy once it's live, so the guide never advises getting around it.
3. **Blog / SEO topic:** "Airbnb's November 2026 house-rule changes: what Australian hosts can still tell guests." High search intent right now. Stick to facts from Airbnb's own help pages, link to them, and keep DigiStayBook to a short mention at the end.
4. **Helpful group answers, not posts about the product.** All three groups ban self-promotion. The user can answer "how do I word this now?" threads with genuine advice (the split between house info and rules) and no link, and only mention DigiStayBook if asked directly. In Hosts Helping Hosts, product mentions only belong in the designated promotion thread, and only if that thread allows services as well as listings. Check before posting.
5. **"Make it personal" content series:** short posts and a landing section on low-effort personal touches for self-check-in stays: a welcome note from the host, local favourites, a guestbook that builds up over time. This matches the small but strong-fit theme and DigiStayBook's core story.

### Draft post 1 (for a "how do I word my house rules now?" thread — reply, no link)

> What's worked for me is separating two things: rules (they go in the listing and have to match Airbnb's policy) and house info (how the place actually works). Bin night, which bin is which, the dishwasher quirk, where the spare blankets are, what to do with the keys. House info written as friendly help rather than "you must" reads completely differently to guests, and most of them are happy to help when you just tell them how things work. Nothing in it should be a condition or carry a fee.

### Draft post 2 (standalone discussion post, where the group allows it — no product mention)

> Question for hosts with self check-in: with the checkout-rule changes coming, I've been rethinking our welcome info. It used to be a list of instructions. Now I'm rewriting it as "here's how the house works, and here are our favourite spots nearby". What do you include that guests actually thank you for? For us it's the wifi on the fridge, where the good coffee is, and a note about the noisy hot-water system so nobody thinks it's broken.
