1Executive summary

Summarise the business, intended customer, core outcome, commercial model, operating approach and reason the plan should proceed.

[Product Name] will be a web-based service for [primary customer]. It will allow a host or organiser to create a managed digital space where guests or contributors can add [text, images, audio and/or video]. Approved content will be available through [a public live wall, a private host view, or both].

The product will operate on a self-service subscription model. Hosts will create and manage their own product spaces, guest contributions will be screened automatically, billing will be handled through Stripe and routine onboarding, notifications, moderation, retention and reporting will be automated wherever practical.

The business will use a shared portfolio architecture based on React, Vite, Firebase, Stripe and GitHub. This approach is intended to keep the product inexpensive to launch, consistent with the wider portfolio and capable of operating with minimal routine manual intervention.

5.2 Live wall and private host view

Explain the supported presentation modes, who can see each mode and the rules governing approved, private, held and unavailable content.

Live wall

Describe the public or shared display experience, supported devices, content ordering, preview behaviour and closed states.

Where enabled, the live wall will display only approved and visible guest content. Firestore real-time updates will add newly approved contributions automatically. A new contribution may be featured briefly before joining the normal display rotation. When the wall contains more content than fits on screen, it will rotate through contributions rather than rely on continuous scrolling.

The wall will support common laptop, television, tablet and phone displays. The host will be able to preview and configure the wall before opening it publicly. If the product is closed, public viewers will receive a clear closed message while the authenticated host retains appropriate preview access.

Private host view

Describe authenticated-only content access, status distinctions and the controls available to authorised Hosts.

Where the product uses a private host view, content will remain visible only to the authenticated host and authorised operators. Approved, held and hidden content will have distinct statuses. Held content will never be presented as approved.

5.4 Content moderation and safety

Document moderation categories, automated and human decisions, fail-closed behaviour, audit records, notifications and escalation rules.

Guest content will be screened automatically before it can appear publicly. Automated moderation will be treated as a risk-control tool rather than a guarantee. Low-risk content will be approved automatically. Uncertain or likely harmful content will be held for review. Clearly disallowed content may be rejected according to the product policy.

Content concernDefault response
Harassment or bullyingHold for review and remove targeted abuse.
Nudity or sexual materialHold. Explicit content is normally rejected for public or family-oriented products.
Obscene or highly offensive materialHold according to the stated audience tolerance.
Threats or violenceHold and escalate serious or credible threats to an internal Platform administrator.
HateHold or reject attacks based on protected characteristics.
Spam, scams or malicious linksReject or quarantine.
Personal or sensitive informationHold before public display.
Illegal or clearly dangerous materialReject and follow the documented incident and legal process.

If moderation is unavailable, new content will remain pending and will not appear on a public wall. A host-only product may retain it privately with a clear held-for-review status. Moderation actions will record the previous status, new status, actor, time and reason. Hosts will receive batched moderation notices rather than one email for every held item.

5.7 Billing operations

Describe checkout, verified fulfilment, renewals, failures, refunds, cancellation, reconciliation and customer self-service.

  • Stripe-hosted checkout will collect payment details. The product will not handle raw card details.
  • Entitlements will be granted or changed only after a signature-verified webhook is processed.
  • Webhook processing will be idempotent so retries cannot duplicate fulfilment.
  • Failed payments will trigger a customer notice, safe retry route and documented grace period.
  • Cancellation, renewal and refunds will be reconciled with the customer's access and communications.
  • Stripe revenue, refunds and disputes will be reviewed against the product database each month.

Material incidents will have a named owner, severity, timeline, customer impact statement and recovery decision. Customers will be notified when the impact is significant or when required by law or policy.

6.2 Authentication and access control

Record the sign-in methods, account and session rules, role model, guest access route, access enforcement and account lifecycle on which the rest of the plan depends.

This section is the single record of how identity works across the product. The decisions below are what will be configured in Firebase Authentication and enforced in Firestore and Storage security rules. Section 2.3 defines what each role is permitted to do, section 6.3.5 specifies the sign-up and sign-in interface and section 5.11 sets the wider privacy and security obligations; each of those relies on the decisions recorded here rather than restating them.

Sign-in methods: record every method as enabled or not enabled. A method that is not enabled must still carry its reason, so the decision is not quietly revisited later.

MethodDecisionReason and operating boundary

Anonymous[Enabled / not enabled]Only for the Guest contribution route where a Guest must not create an account; see the guest access decision below.

Account and session rules: these determine how an identity is trusted over time and which actions require fresh proof of it.

RequirementDecision to recordImportant boundary

Password policy[Minimum length, complexity and reuse rules]Stated on the sign-up page before submission rather than only in an error message.
Account recovery[Reset route, link expiry and reuse rules]Recovery messages must not disclose whether an account exists.

Abuse protection[Rate limits, lockout behaviour and authorised domains]Authorised domains must be restricted before launch so credentials cannot be used from unknown origins.

Roles and how they are granted: the roles below are those defined in section 2.3. This table records how each one is established and withdrawn in practice.

RoleHow identity is establishedHow the role is granted and removed

Guest access: the Guest contribution route will use [an anonymous authentication session, a signed link with a server-verified token, or an unauthenticated write handled entirely by a Function]. Whichever route is selected, a Guest must never gain read access to host records, held content or operator information, and the route must remain usable without registration. Where anonymous sessions are used, the plan must also state how abandoned anonymous accounts are cleaned up.

Access enforcement: the following must hold before launch, and are the conditions the security rules are written against.

  • Every application record carries an explicit owner field, and rules compare it with the authenticated account identifier rather than any client-supplied value
  • Storage paths are product-owned, with write access limited by size, content type and the contribution route in force
  • Roles are carried as verified custom claims or server-held records, never as client state, and are re-checked on every privileged operation
  • Billing, moderation, email and administrative operations run only in Firebase Functions against a verified identity token
  • The public wall exposes approved content only, and fails closed where status cannot be confirmed
  • Platform administrator access uses a separate protected route with multi-factor authentication and an explicit account allowlist
  • Rules are exercised for host, guest, public and unauthorised access before launch, as required by section 6.6

Account lifecycle: the plan must state what happens to the authentication record when a Host cancels, is disabled or requests deletion, including whether the identity is retained for billing history, at what point the Firebase Authentication user is removed, and what becomes of the product spaces that account owns. These outcomes must match the retention and deletion commitments in section 5.10.
