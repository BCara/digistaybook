1Executive summary

DigiStayBook is a web-based digital guestbook for short-term rental hosts. A host creates a property in their dashboard, seeds it with a welcome post and house information, and receives a printed QR placard to put on the kitchen counter. Guests scan it during their stay, read the host’s pinned house information, and add photos and messages to a shared wall — without downloading an app, creating an account or entering a password. The wall stays live for as long as the host maintains the subscription.

The product is sold per property on a self-service subscription: a monthly plan or a discounted annual plan, both including the full feature set, with a 28-day free trial on the host’s first activated property. Payment, renewal, dunning and cancellation run through Stripe. Guest submissions are screened automatically before they can reach a public wall, and only genuinely severe cases reach a person inside the business.

The service is designed so that the host’s workload after setup is close to zero, and so that the business’s workload per customer is close to zero. There is deliberately no guest-to-host messaging, no maintenance ticketing and no in-stay guest support: those belong to the host’s booking provider, and building them would import an operational burden the business cannot carry.

Delivery uses React, TypeScript and Vite on Firebase (Authentication, Firestore, Storage, Functions, Hosting) with Stripe for payments and an email provider behind an adapter boundary. This keeps the product inexpensive to launch, consistent with the wider portfolio, and capable of running with minimal routine manual intervention.

1.1 Business objective

The initial objective is to prove that short-term rental hosts will pay a small recurring fee, per property, for a guest experience that is warmer and more useful than an automated check-in message and a laminated house-rules sheet — and to do so within an operating model one operator and one developer can sustain alongside the rest of the portfolio.

Progress is demonstrated by evidence, not activity. The milestones that count are: a host completing sign-up, property creation and paid activation without assistance; a guest scanning a printed placard and posting a memory without support; a first trial converting to a paid subscription; and a first annual renewal charged and retained.

2Business overview

DigiStayBook sits between a host and their guests for the duration of a stay. It does not sit between a host and their booking provider, and it does not take on the guest relationship.

2.1 Product

The product provides a per-property digital guestbook, reached by a printed QR code inside the property. Each property has:

  • A Guest Wall: a mobile-first, reverse-chronological feed of approved guest photos and messages, with host-pinned content held at the top.
  • A digital house manual, delivered through the same pinned posts — Wi-Fi details, appliance instructions, bin days, local recommendations and the checkout routine — so that stay information is available at the moment a guest needs it rather than buried in a booking-app thread.
  • A host dashboard covering property setup, theme and layout, content curation, pinning, the moderation and takedown queues, private guest feedback, QR assets and billing.
  • A printed QR kit: high-resolution placard artwork issued on activation, plus a shareable live routing link.

A guest can add up to ten images in a single post, edit or delete their own contributions, copy the text of a post and report any post. A guest can also leave one-way, text-only private feedback to the host. Nothing in the product carries a guest message to a host’s inbox, and nothing invites a guest into a conversation.

2.2 Customer

The paying customer is the short-term rental host or operator: an individual owner with one property, or a small operator running several. They already use a booking provider such as Airbnb, Vrbo or Booking.com for reservations, payments, messaging and reviews. Their problem is not distribution — it is that the stay itself has become impersonal, that house information is fragmented, and that the same questions arrive by message for every booking. They have very little tolerance for another dashboard to check, and none at all for a product that generates support work.

The guest is not the customer and is never asked to become one. They are in an unfamiliar house for a few nights, holding a phone, with no reason to install anything. The product must be usable by a guest who has never heard of DigiStayBook, in under a minute, on a phone with poor signal, without registering.

2.4 Product scope

The first release includes: host account creation; multi-property management from a single dashboard; the pre-purchase sandbox and demo wall; anti-blank-wall seeding; per-property activation through Stripe checkout with trial and coupon support; printed QR kit generation and live routing links; the Guest Wall with pinning and themes; guest contribution with consent capture; automated content screening with host review and internal escalation; the report and takedown state machine; private guest feedback; the transactional and consented-marketing email lifecycle; and the retention and deletion schedule.

The first release explicitly excludes:

  • Guest-to-host messaging. There is no reply channel. Private feedback is one-way and is not monitored in real time.
  • Maintenance reporting or issue ticketing. Stay problems belong to the booking provider.
  • Guest stay support of any kind from DigiStayBook. The single exception is privacy, personal-data and platform content-safety matters, which are handled through the public Privacy & Safety route.
  • Any review-generation function. No rating request, no review-transfer instruction, no pre-filled review text, no deep link into a provider review form, and no feature or benefit conditioned on leaving a review.
  • Guest accounts and app downloads. Access is by printed QR code and browser only.
  • In-property tablet hardware. The MVP is physical-QR-first precisely to avoid Wi-Fi, battery and device-maintenance dependencies.

Later additions are held in Appendix A.8 and will be considered only after the core journey and willingness to pay have been demonstrated.

3Market position

3.1 Customer problem

Short-term rental hosting has become efficient and impersonal at the same time. Self check-in, keypad locks and automated messaging mean a guest can arrive, stay and leave without a single human exchange. The stay works, but it feels like a transaction in an empty house.

That produces three practical problems for the host:

  • The stay has no character. Nothing in the property tells the guest that someone cares about it, and nothing invites the guest to treat it as more than a room they paid for.
  • House information is fragmented and arrives at the wrong time. Wi-Fi passwords, bin nights, appliance quirks and checkout steps are spread across the booking-app thread, a printed folder nobody opens and a welcome message read three weeks before arrival. The same questions then arrive by message, per booking, forever.
  • There is nothing that accumulates. Every stay starts from zero. A guestbook on the hall table does accumulate, but only the next guest at that table can see it, and it cannot be moderated, backed up or removed on request.

The impact is a stay that is competent but forgettable, and a support load that never falls. Hosts care because guest experience is the one lever they control that a booking provider does not set for them.

4.2 Revenue model

Revenue is recurring subscription income, earned per activated property. Stripe Checkout collects payment, Stripe Billing manages the recurring subscription and the trial, and the Stripe Customer Portal handles self-service payment-method changes. The product never handles raw card details.

The commercial boundary is enforced at activation. A host may create an account, create a property, use the pre-purchase sandbox, upload custom photos, write welcome notes and preview every theme without paying. What stays locked until a valid payment method is authorised — or a 100% discount code is confirmed — is the live routing URL and the downloadable high-resolution QR kit. This is the revenue guardrail: the host can see the whole product before paying, but nothing reaches a guest until the property is activated.

Entitlements change only when a signature-verified Stripe webhook is processed. Nothing in the client grants access.

Checkout must display the exact terms before purchase, with the trial end date calculated and shown as a date rather than as “in 28 days”, and a required consent checkbox next to the disclaimer reading: “I understand I will be billed ${Price} on {Date} if I do not cancel.” The monthly and annual variants of this microcopy are held in section 6.3.4.

4.3 Commercial principles

  • Marketing, the pricing page, checkout, dashboard entitlements and the customer policies describe the same offer, in the same words, at the same price.
  • Limits are visible before they are reached, and the exact reason is shown when an action is blocked.
  • Cancellation is self-service in the dashboard and nowhere else. Cancellation requests are not accepted by email or contact form, because neither route can verify account ownership.
  • On cancellation the host keeps full access to the wall and QR code until the end of the paid cycle or trial, and is told the exact date access ends and what happens to the content afterwards.
  • Charges are non-refundable except where required by law, including a major failure under the Australian Consumer Law. Statutory rights are never described as a goodwill concession.
  • Goodwill refunds outside the policy require a recorded decision by a named owner and are reconciled against Stripe.
  • The business does not promise unlimited storage, indefinite retention, original-quality media, guaranteed uptime or any review, revenue or property outcome unless the promise is implemented, costed and substantiated.
  • A failed payment never produces a broken link for a guest standing in the property. It produces a designed fallback page. Protecting the host’s appearance in front of their own guests is a commercial rule, not a courtesy.

5.2 Guest Wall and private host views

The Guest Wall (public)

The Guest Wall is mobile-first and built for a phone browser held in a kitchen. It renders the property’s selected theme and layout, with host-pinned and featured content held permanently at the top and all other approved posts flowing beneath in reverse-chronological order, newest first. A guest who has just posted sees their contribution immediately, which is the confirmation that the thing worked.

Only approved, visible content is eligible for public display. Held, hidden, quarantined and privately delivered content is never rendered publicly, and the wall fails closed: where a post’s status cannot be confirmed, it is not shown.

Every live public wall, including the public demo, carries a discreet text link after the wall content and contribution controls: “Loved your stay? Powered by DigiStayBook — Create a digital guestbook for your property.” It links to the DigiStayBook landing page with a descriptive accessible label. It must remain visually secondary to the property’s identity, and it must never appear as a popup, interstitial, modal, forced redirect or contribution requirement.

When a property is suspended for non-payment or has been cancelled, the QR code does not break. It routes to a designed fallback page stating that the property’s digital guestbook is temporarily offline. No error page, no 404, nothing that embarrasses the host in front of a guest standing in their kitchen.

Private host views

Two surfaces are authenticated-only and never public:

  • The preview. The host can see the wall as a guest would, in any theme, before activation and afterwards. Preview and public modes must be visibly distinguishable so a host never mistakes one for the other.
  • The private feedback inbox. One-way, text-only guest feedback is logged silently for the host and is never rendered on the public wall, never emailed as content, and never given a reply channel.

Approved, held, hidden and pending-deletion content carry distinct statuses in the dashboard. Held content is never displayed to the host as approved.

5.3 Guest content

The contribution route is reached only by scanning the property’s printed QR code or opening its live routing link. It is mobile-first, requires no account, and asks for nothing about the guest beyond what the contribution itself contains.

Before submitting, a guest sees the property identity, the host’s instructions and writing prompt, the permitted content types and limits, and a mandatory single-tap consent checkbox immediately above the submit button: “I agree to the Guest Terms and Privacy Policy, consent to this image being displayed publicly, and confirm I am over 16 years of age.” That consent is recorded with a timestamp, the exact wording and its version, and a pseudonymous post and session reference — without creating an account and without duplicating the photograph into the consent record.

5.4 Content moderation and safety

Every guest submission is screened before it can appear publicly. Automated screening is a risk-control tool, not a guarantee, and is never described as one.

Quarantine-first architecture

On submission, files are uploaded to a private, non-public storage bucket and the post is assigned a processing status. While the guest sees a brief optimistic loading state, a dual-layer server-side scan runs: image moderation and concurrent text filtering, including string matching for profanity, sentiment analysis and URL detection. The scan looks only for unsafe or explicit content. No biometric data and no facial-recognition template is generated, extracted or stored from a guest image — a hard constraint driven by biometric privacy law, not a preference.

Fail-closed behaviour

If the moderation provider is unavailable, new content remains pending and does not appear on a public wall. It is not published optimistically and reviewed later.

Internal review cadence

  • The daily sweep. Operations reviews the critical escalation queue once per day. That queue holds only AI-flagged severe cases and host-initiated escalations — never ordinary curation. Each case is resolved by restricting the content and session where proportionate, retaining a restricted case only for the approved review period, deleting securely when review and legal-retention needs end, or downgrading a false positive to host review.

  • Unresolved critical cases are reviewed at least every 30 days.

  • A reporter enters a 24-hour suspected-abuse cooldown after reporting 3 distinct posts on one wall within 10 minutes, or 5 distinct posts within 24 hours.
  • A wall-level breaker trips when 5 distinct posts are reported within 10 minutes across related reporter signals. An internal reviewer can still restrict a credible urgent safety report while the breaker is active.
  • Two internally confirmed malicious-reporting cases within 90 days may create a proportionate restriction lasting no more than 90 days, linked only to a keyed hash of the existing Firebase anonymous or account UID. No new cross-device identity is created, and a cleared browser session cannot be correlated through an invented device fingerprint. Restrictions expire automatically.

Nothing is silently dropped. Every syntactically valid report receives the same neutral acknowledgement: “Report received. It will be reviewed.” The client never claims that deletion was approved and never reveals whether visibility changed. Reports received during a circuit-breaker state are logged, acknowledged, routed for internal review and left visible.

  • Reviews supplier invoices and usage against the drivers in 8.1, in particular gigabytes stored, moderation calls and emails sent.
  • Calculates revenue and direct contribution margin per active property.
  • Identifies unexpected cost growth and its cause — a single heavy property, a retry loop, an unexpected traffic source.
  • Reviews trial-to-paid conversion, monthly-to-annual mix, cancellations and the reasons given.
  • Records the next pricing, limit or supplier decision, with an owner and a date.

Held deliberately outside the launch scope in section 2.4. Each of these is built only after the core journey and willingness to pay have been demonstrated, and each is judged on whether it adds operational load the business cannot carry.

A.8.1 Affiliate and referral engine

Goal: an attributable host-acquisition channel through peer and industry referrals, without promising a particular conversion or revenue outcome.

Mechanism: affiliates generate leads through unique tracking links. Commission is paid only on annual subscription sales, at 40% of the first year’s revenue — so the payout scales with localised pricing automatically. Paying only on annual sales protects upfront cash flow.

The payout notification is transactional and contains payment facts only. Any request to share again or promote further is a separate marketing message requiring recorded consent, a send-time suppression check, sender details and unsubscribe.

A.8.2 Contextual location-based writing prompts

Goal: make it easier for a guest to start writing. This may support participation; no upload-rate increase is promised.

Mechanism: the host selects a property archetype — default, beach, mountain or country, urban — in settings. The contribution page then shows location-aware placeholder text, for example “Our favourite hiking trail nearby was…” for a mountain property.

A.8.3 Dual-carousel tablet display

Mechanism: a dedicated interface for hosts who choose to run a tablet on the counter: a left carousel for host-pinned guidelines, a right carousel for live posts, and a persistent on-screen QR code so guests still contribute from their own phones.

This deliberately reverses the MVP’s physical-QR-first constraint, so it carries the hardware dependencies that constraint exists to avoid: Wi-Fi reconnection, battery management and device maintenance, all of which land on the host. It should be offered only where a host has asked for it.

Mechanism: use standard mobile browser translation APIs to detect the guest’s language and offer a lightweight toggle that translates the host’s pinned text — the checkout guide and how-to posts — into it.
