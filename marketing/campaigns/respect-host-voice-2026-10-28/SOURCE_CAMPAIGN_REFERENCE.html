A home with a host

Let guests see the care. Give them a clear way to care for it.

A host-first campaign about guests respecting the home and hosts telling its story in their own voice. Four weeks of ideas, with the first week built as downloadable content.

The creative direction

Make the person behind the property visible.

Lead with the person and the home, then offer useful wording, then show the product. Respect is the aim; clear guidance and a recognisable host are the mechanisms we can actually demonstrate.

“Help guests feel at home — and understand what makes it yours.”

Who this is for

Australian owner-hosts of one or a few cottages, cabins and holiday homes who care about the place and want their welcome to sound like them.

What the product gives them

Your welcome, your house guidance and your own memory wall, in a browser guests can open from your property QR display.

What we want them to do

Primary action: explore the guest demo. Next action: create an account and add one property with a welcome note and one practical house essential. Do not make an unverified time-to-setup or outcome promise.

Define “control your narrative” honestly.

Hosts write their own welcome and guidance, add their own notes, curate the memories on their DigiStayBook wall, and can switch on approval for new memories. The public memory page can be switched off. Separate private feedback gives guests another route to the host. This does not change reviews on booking platforms or guarantee how guests behave.

Voice: warm, specific and confident. Show care and clear boundaries without shaming guests. Use illustrative wording honestly. No invented host testimonials, incident statistics, scarcity, discounts or guaranteed outcomes.

Facebook

Conversation and product understanding

Longer host-first captions, one multi-image writing lesson, one actual product Reel, one open question and one worksheet. Use the Page feed; no group posting or outreach is included.

Pinterest

Useful material worth saving

Searchable titles, vertical writing guides and checklists, each with its own destination. Treat the first month as a catalogue-building experiment; no instant traffic promise.

TikTok (optional)

Demonstrate one idea quickly

Two captioned 9:16 exports with different hooks. Account not yet established. No TikTok-specific performance claim or dependency in the main plan.

Variety is part of the plan.

The first week has six visual treatments: home photography, a before/after writing lesson, product screens, a type-led question, a garden note and a writing worksheet. Only Tuesday’s wording lesson and Wednesday’s demonstration are a deliberate two-part sequence. Pins are their own practical guides, with unique titles and copy. Story backgrounds vary with the daily subject.

Your first welcome, in three lines

Try this before you add another house rule.

Write three short lines:
1. “Welcome to…” — the feeling you want guests to have.
2. “One thing we love about this home…” — a detail only you could share.
3. “Please help us look after…” — one clear request, with a reason.

For example:
“Welcome to our little garden cottage. We planted the herb beds during our first winter here. Please snip a few leaves for dinner and leave the roots growing for the next guests.”

That is example copy, not a customer story. Keep the welcome brief, then put practical instructions in your house essentials and host notes.

How to run it

A useful first-month experiment.

Cadence: Five Facebook feed posts (including one Reel), seven lightweight Stories, five Pinterest Pins, and two optional TikTok exports. Thursday and Saturday are Facebook feed rest days. Daily Stories are optional if capacity is tight.

Timing: Suggested test slots in Sydney time: Facebook feed 7:30 pm; Stories 8:00 am; Pinterest 8:30 pm. These are practical starting slots, not researched best times. Leave TikTok unscheduled until the account and profile destination are ready.

Budget: Organic-first. No paid spend, publishing, scheduling, outreach or account creation included.

Track: Maintain a simple dated log per creative: published URL, impressions/reach, useful host comments, saves, outbound clicks, and attributable account/property setup where tracking actually exists. Use UTM links; do not assume the full setup funnel is instrumented.

The hypothesis: Working creative hypothesis, not a forecast: host recognition earns attention; useful wording earns saves; real product demonstrations earn demo visits.

At the end of week one: At the end of week one, choose the next two pieces using the strongest useful signal: real host questions, saves and demo clicks. Expand a useful topic into a new example and layout; do not clone the top-performing image. Low response is a reason to test the hook or destination, not proof that the product does not work.

Month-end evidence: Desired month-end evidence: identifiable host needs, people exploring the demo, and hosts trying a first-property welcome. No claim that post engagement demonstrates more respectful guest behaviour.

Community replies

“Does it stop guests damaging a home?”No guest guide can guarantee that. DigiStayBook gives your welcome and practical instructions a clear place to live, so guests can understand how you would like the home looked after.
“Can I control my reviews?”You control the welcome, guidance and memories shown on your DigiStayBook wall, including an option to approve new memories. Reviews on booking platforms stay on those platforms.
“Do guests need another app?”No. Guests open DigiStayBook in their browser from the property QR display. They do not need a guest account.
“Can people leave private feedback?”Yes. There is a separate private-feedback form for the host. It does not replace messages or reviews through the booking platform.
“Do I have to approve every memory?”You can switch on approval for new memories. When it is off, clear memories can publish after safety checks.
A host shares a blunt house ruleThanks for the example. You could try: “[clear request], because [true reason]. You will find [practical help] at [actual location].” Keep the boundary and make it easy to follow.
A host shares an upsetting guest incidentThat sounds frustrating. Please avoid posting guest names or identifying details here. What one instruction would you want the next guest to find more easily?
Someone asks for pricingThe current plans are shown on the DigiStayBook pricing page. Check the live page before quoting a price or trial offer. No price or limited offer is part of this campaign.
