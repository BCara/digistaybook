# Paper guestbook, digital guidebook or guest journal? An honest comparison for vacation rental hosts

Most vacation rentals welcome guests in one of three ways: a binder and guestbook on the counter, a digital guidebook sent as a
link or QR code, or not much at all. A newer option, the digital guest journal, lets guests leave something behind for the next
people who stay.

We make DigiStayBook, so we aren't neutral. What we can do is be specific. Below we compare DigiStayBook with three tools hosts
often weigh it against (Touch Stay, Hostfully Digital Guidebooks and StayPage) and with the classic paper binder, using only what
each company says on its own website as of November 1, 2026. Where another tool does something we don't, we say so.

## Two jobs: informing guests and collecting memories

A guest welcome does two different jobs.

**Informing.** Where's the Wi-Fi password? Which day is trash pickup? How does the heating work? What's the checkout routine?
Digital guidebooks such as Touch Stay and Hostfully are built for this, and they do it well.

**Collecting.** A guestbook gathers the notes, photos and local finds guests leave behind. Paper guestbooks do this, and digital
guest journals such as StayPage do it on a phone. StayPage's own FAQ is clear that it isn't a guidebook.

**DigiStayBook does both from one QR code.** Guests scan a placard and open a page in their phone's browser. Your house guidance
(Wi-Fi, trash day, appliances, checkout) and your local favorites sit at the top. Below them is a wall of photos and notes from
earlier guests, so every stay adds to the story of your place.

## Touch Stay

Touch Stay is a dedicated digital guidebook. Its site describes guidebooks that are "mobile-first, with no app required," shared
by direct link, QR code or automated messages through a property management system. It lists auto-translation in 30+ languages,
40+ integrations, a built-in upsell store and a way to capture private guest feedback.

**Where it's stronger:** translation, integrations and selling extras. If you host many international guests or want your guide
sent automatically from your property management system, Touch Stay covers ground DigiStayBook doesn't. It has a free plan
(limited to 20 guest views per guidebook per month) and a 14-day free trial.

**What's different:** Touch Stay is about the host informing the guest. We didn't see a place for guests to leave photos or notes
for the next guest.

## Hostfully Digital Guidebooks

Hostfully is best known for property management software, and its guidebooks plug into it. Your first guidebook is free, with
"no credit cards." Guidebooks offer auto-translation in 16+ languages, can pull recommendations and maps from Google, and can be
shared as a private link, a PDF or, according to Hostfully's help center, a QR code. A Guidebook Marketplace lets hosts offer paid
extras such as early check-in or late checkout.

**Where it's stronger:** if you already run your rentals on Hostfully, a guidebook in the same system is hard to beat, and the
free first guidebook is an easy way to try a digital guide.

**What's different:** the guidebook content comes from the host. We didn't see a way for guests to add their own photos or notes.

## StayPage

StayPage is closest to DigiStayBook's guestbook side. It calls itself "a guest journal for your vacation home": guests scan a QR
card and leave stories, photos and local finds from their phone browser, "with no app, account, or password." Hosts can publish
pages instantly or hold each one for approval, and hide any page.

**Where it's stronger:** if you only want a guest journal and your house information already lives somewhere else, StayPage is
focused on exactly that job, and its Starter plan is free.

**What's different:** you'd still need a guidebook or binder for the Wi-Fi, checkout and the rest. DigiStayBook puts the practical
answers and the guestbook behind the same code, so guests who open it for the Wi-Fi also see what earlier guests loved.

## The paper binder and guestbook

Plenty of lovely rentals still run on paper, and it has real charm. A handwritten note carries a warmth no screen quite matches,
and paper needs no Wi-Fi or battery.

The trade-offs are familiar. When the Wi-Fi password or trash day changes, pages need reprinting. A guestbook fills up, gets coffee
on it or leaves in a suitcase, and guests can only read what's on the shelf. And there's no easy way to tell the host privately
that something needs fixing while the stay is still underway.

## Where DigiStayBook fits

DigiStayBook is built around one idea: a rental should feel like staying in someone's home, not a business.

- **Guests find answers themselves.** Wi-Fi, trash day, appliance quirks and checkout are on the counter, on their phone. No app,
  no guest account. The placard can carry a Wi-Fi code that a phone joins with one scan, and the wall copies the password in a tap.
- **A guestbook that builds up instead of filling up.** Each stay can add photos, notes and local tips alongside your own welcome.
- **You stay in control.** Posts pass automated screening, borderline ones wait for your approval, and you can remove anything.
  Screening reduces risk; no system can promise every post is perfect, which is why you have the final say.
- **Hear about problems while you can fix them.** Guests can send you private feedback that's never published, so you hear about
  the slow shower during the stay. It isn't a review and doesn't replace one.
- **Update once, every guest sees it.** Change the Wi-Fi password on your phone and the next guest sees the new one.
- **Share the memories.** A separate [public page](/wall/demo-cottage) shows guest photos and notes, without house details, to share with past guests
  or embed on your own website.
- **One price per property, everything included**, with a 28-day free trial on your first property. [See pricing](/pricing).

## When DigiStayBook isn't the right fit

We'd rather you pick the right tool. DigiStayBook probably isn't for you if you need your guide in several languages, want it sent
automatically from your property management system, want to sell extras to guests, or don't want guest posts at all. The guest
wall is at the heart of DigiStayBook; if a guestbook isn't part of your plan, a plain guidebook may suit you better.

## Who each option suits

- **Touch Stay:** hosts and managers who want a full-featured guidebook with translation, integrations and upsells.
- **Hostfully Digital Guidebooks:** hosts already on Hostfully's software, or anyone who wants to try one guidebook free.
- **StayPage:** hosts who want a guest journal only, with house information handled elsewhere.
- **Paper:** hosts who love handwriting and rarely change their details.
- **DigiStayBook:** independent hosts and small portfolios who want practical answers and a growing guestbook behind one QR code,
  with screening and private feedback included.

## Questions hosts ask

**Do guests need an app or an account?**
No. Guests scan the placard and DigiStayBook opens in their browser. There's no guest account; a name on a post is optional, and
we don't ask guests for an email address or phone number.

**Can I use DigiStayBook alongside another guidebook?**
Yes. Some hosts may keep an existing guidebook and add DigiStayBook for the guest wall and private feedback. Just keep the details
in both consistent.

**Can I try it first?**
Yes. [See what guests see](/stay/demo-cottage) on our demo property, or make a free [QR welcome sign](/qr-sign-maker), no account
needed.
