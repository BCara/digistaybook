Why hosts choose us

This is the heart of all DigiStayBook marketing. Every post, ad, page and email should land on the core reason or one of the reasons below, and respect the guardrails. The marketing plan page shows this section as "Why hosts choose us". Agreed 29 October 2026.

The core reason

It makes a rental feel like staying in someone's home, not a business. One QR code gives guests what they need, when they need it, and lets them leave something for the next guest. Guidebooks only inform, paper guestbooks only collect, and doing nothing is the real competitor.

  • Line: "Give your guests a welcome, not a laminated sheet."

1. Guests can find everything themselves

Wi-Fi, trash days, heating and checkout are answered on the counter, on their phone, without messaging you. No app, no account, under a minute in a browser tab.

  • Line: "The answers are on the counter, not in your inbox."
  • Guardrail: never promise fewer messages or less support work.

2. Guests help because they feel welcome, not because they're told to

Airbnb's new house-rules policy (bookings made on or after 20 November 2026) limits what hosts may require: no taking trash off-site, stripping beds, laundry or washing dishes. Respectful guests still help when they feel they are staying in someone's home.

  • Line: "The rules limit what you can ask. They don't limit how welcome you can make someone feel."
  • Guardrail: the wall gives information ("the trash cans are the black ones at the blue gate, pickup is Tuesday"), never rules or requirements. Airbnb says extra rules shouldn't appear on signs in the home. Never pitch DigiStayBook as a way around the rules.
  • Guardrail: don't name Airbnb, or any booking platform, in paid ads, and never imply an endorsement or partnership.

3. A guestbook that builds up instead of filling up

Every stay adds photos, notes and local tips for the next guest. A paper book fills up and gets lost; a guidebook only talks at guests. Tips from people who actually stayed carry more weight than the host's own list.

  • Line: "Every stay adds to the story of your place."

4. You stay in control of what's shown

Posts are screened automatically, borderline ones wait for the host's approval, and the host can remove anything.

  • Line: "Nothing questionable lands on your wall unreviewed."
  • Guardrail: screening reduces risk; never say it guarantees safe content.

5. A small, simple cost that could be a business expense

One price per property, everything included, a 28-day free trial and one-click cancellation. Lead boldly on tax, the way Officeworks does ("could be tax deductible"), and qualify in the small print.

  • Lines: "Claim it at tax time." / "Could be a 100% business expense."
  • Small print, every time: "DigiStayBook could be tax deductible as a business expense for your rental. Eligibility depends on your circumstances; check with your tax adviser."
  • Guardrail: never "is tax deductible", "highly likely", "in most cases", or a figure for what someone will save.
  • Guardrail: no currency in marketing (see "A global audience" below). Say "one low monthly price per property" or point to the pricing page, which shows each visitor their own currency.

6. Repeat guests can find you directly (hold until live)

Guests already have the wall open for the Wi-Fi, so a "Stay with us again" link to the host's own booking page or guest list is right there. Posters and business cards don't get that reach.

  • Line: "Your next booking, one tap from the Wi-Fi."
  • Status: built on 29 October 2026, not deployed yet. Don't market it until it is live.
  • Guardrail: our copy never says "book direct and save" or offers a discount for booking off a platform (Airbnb bans this), and never promises repeat bookings or lower commission. What each host's link says and offers is up to them.

7. Hear about problems while you can still fix them

Guests can send the host private feedback that is never published: the slow shower, the missing pan, the blind that sticks. Hosts are anxious about reviews, and this gives them the chance to fix things during the stay.

  • Line: "Hear about the slow shower while you can still fix it."
  • Guardrail: never suggest it replaces, prevents or heads off reviews. Booking platforms ban review manipulation, and we never ask guests for ratings or steer them away from reviewing.

8. Real moments from real stays, on your own website

The public wall can be embedded on the host's own site or shared with past guests, so a direct-booking site carries real guest photos and notes.

  • Line: "Real moments from real stays, on your own website."
  • Guardrail: they are memories, not reviews or ratings. Don't suggest putting the link in a booking-platform listing: Airbnb bans listing links that take people off Airbnb.

9. Guests get online first

The QR placard can carry a Wi-Fi code that a phone joins with one scan, and the wall copies the password in one tap. Guests without mobile data (international visitors especially) aren't stuck before they can read anything.

  • Line: "Scan, connect, settle in."

10. Update it once, every guest sees it

Change the Wi-Fi password, trash day or a local tip on the wall and the next guest sees it. No reprinting, no out-of-date folder.

  • Line: "Change the Wi-Fi password once. Every guest sees it."

11. No guest accounts, no data grab

Guests don't sign up, and DigiStayBook doesn't collect their contact details. That's easier for guests and in line with the wider move, including Airbnb's new rules, against hosts gathering guest data.

  • Line: "No app. No account. No catch."
  • Guardrail: describe what we don't collect; don't give privacy or legal advice.

Timely hooks

  • Airbnb's chore rules (bookings from November 20, 2026): "Rewrite your checkout notes for the new rules": useful content leading into reason 2. Link to Airbnb's own page; never interpret the policy for hosts.
  • The over-automation backlash: "Automate the logistics. Keep the welcome human."
  • A crowded US market: "In a crowded market, be the stay they remember." No promised bookings or reviews.
  • Direct booking, once reason 6 is live: Airbnb is reportedly piloting a lower host fee for guests who book through the host's own Airbnb link. A host could put that link in their "Stay with us again" card and stay within Airbnb's rules. Unconfirmed pilot: check the details before using it.

Careful with

  • Local posting laws: many US cities require the emergency contact, occupancy limit and trash rules to be physically posted inside the rental. Say the wall also carries this information; never that it meets a legal requirement.
  • Translation: not built. Don't market multilingual guides.

Lines we never use

  • Better reviews or star ratings. Approved instead: "A better stay may encourage more positive feedback on the booking platform."
  • Less damage, fewer messages, more revenue, or any connection with, or endorsement by, a booking platform.
  • "Is tax deductible", "highly likely" or "in most cases".
  • Any currency symbol, code or amount.

Which reason for which audience

  • Self-check-in hosts: reasons 1, 2, 9 and 10.
  • Hosts who care about character and reviews: reasons 3, 4 and 7.
  • Price-sensitive hosts, and US tax season (January to April): reason 5.
  • Experienced hosts building direct bookings (for example the "Hosts Helping Hosts" Facebook group): reason 8 now, reason 6 once live.
  • Property managers with several homes: reasons 4, 10 and 5 (one price per property, one dashboard).
  • Hosts hosting international guests: reason 9.
